A typical iGaming call centre listens to about 2.6% of its calls. The rest — including the ones where a player in distress gets no responsible-gaming response — are never heard. Why manual call QA covers only about 2.6% of calls

Manual call QA covers so little because listening is slow: a specialist needs about 7.5 minutes per call to listen, take notes and fill in a scorecard. One person can therefore review roughly 64 calls in a working day. Calls do not compress the way chats do — you cannot skim a recording the way you skim a transcript.

Take a mid-sized casino or sportsbook call centre handling 5,000 calls a day, inbound and outbound together. Here is what manual QA looks like at that volume:

The last two rows are the point. Nobody hires 78 people to listen to calls, so every operator settles for a sample. And a 2.6% sample is not a quality programme — it is a lottery. Rude replies, wrong answers about withdrawals and missed compliance wording in the other 97% stay invisible until a complaint, a chargeback or a regulator surfaces them.

What AI call QA does

AI call QA turns every recorded call into text, scores it against your standards and points to the exact moment each problem happened. In QODEQ AI Voice QA this happens in four steps:

  1. Transcription. Every inbound and outbound call is transcribed with Whisper into searchable text, so calls can be filtered and searched like chats.
  2. Scoring on 15 criteria. The transcript is scored on the same 15 weighted criteria as chat QA, calibrated to your call scripts — greeting and identification, problem intake, empathy and hold handling, compliance wording, close and next steps.
  3. Breach timestamps. Each flag carries the exact second it occurred, ready for a coaching session: the team lead opens the call at 02:14, not at 00:00.
  4. Reports. Scores roll up by operator, shift and call type, so trends show up in days, not quarters.

Which criteria to score on iGaming calls

The criteria should mirror your call script, step by step, plus the compliance points you are accountable for. Each of the 15 criteria gets a weight, so a missed compliance step costs more points than a slightly informal greeting. A typical iGaming call scorecard groups criteria like this:

GroupExample criteriaWhat a breach looks like
OpeningGreeting, player identification, call purposeAccount details discussed before the caller is identified
IntakeProblem captured, context checkedAgent answers a different question from the one asked
AccuracyAnswer matches rules and knowledge baseWrong withdrawal timeframe or bonus condition quoted
ConductEmpathy, tone, hold handlingLong silence with no explanation; dismissive tone
ComplianceResponsible-gaming trigger, required wordingPlayer mentions losses, no protocol offered
ClosingResolution, next steps, summaryCall ends without agreed next step

Weights are set in week 2 of rollout and adjusted after the parallel run. Most teams start from their existing manual scorecard — the AI does not need a new methodology, it needs the one you already use, applied to every call.

Two practical tips. First, keep criteria observable: "offered self-exclusion options when the player asked to stop" can be checked in a transcript, "made the player feel valued" cannot. Second, separate scripts for inbound, VIP and outbound calls — what counts as a breach on a support call may be required wording on a campaign call.

Why responsible-gaming wording on calls matters

Responsible-gaming conversations often happen by phone: a player asks to lower limits, mentions losses, or wants to close an account. Operators define their own protocol for these moments — what the agent must say, offer and record. With AI call QA, that protocol becomes one of the scoring criteria, so every call where it should have been triggered is checked, not just the few that a reviewer happened to hear. Your compliance team still owns the protocol; the system makes adherence measurable.

Inbound support, VIP and outbound calls

AI call QA works on every call type you record, because it scores transcripts against the scripts you define for each type. In iGaming that typically covers three streams:

  • Inbound support — withdrawals, deposits, KYC, account blocks and bonus questions. Criteria focus on identification, accuracy and resolution.
  • VIP and retention calls — the highest-value conversations, where tone and follow-through matter most. Full coverage means no VIP call goes unchecked.
  • Outbound campaigns — registration follow-ups, win-back and payout confirmation. Criteria check that offers and compliance wording are stated exactly as scripted.

If outbound calls are made by an AI agent rather than people, see AI voice bots in iGaming — the bot follows the script by design, and QA focuses on the human-handled calls.

What changes for the QA team

The QA team stops sampling and starts coaching. With reviews taking 5–15 seconds per call, the 1–2 specialists who spent their day listening to 128 recordings move to work that improves operators:

  • Calibration — checking AI scores against their own judgement and adjusting criterion weights.
  • Coaching — using breach timestamps to run short, specific sessions with each operator.
  • Trend analysis — spotting which scripts, shifts or call types produce the most breaches.
  • Compliance evidence — showing that every call was checked against the protocol, not a sample.

The specialists do not disappear — their time moves from collecting data to acting on it. That is where call QA actually pays off.

Security and data handling

Call recordings contain personal and financial data, so they are handled with stricter rules than chat logs. QODEQ AI Voice QA processes recordings locally, keeps them encrypted and works in line with GDPR. Storage rules are agreed during telephony integration in week 1, before any recording is processed.

— FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is AI call QA?

AI call QA is automated quality assurance for phone calls: every recording is transcribed, scored against your criteria and flagged with timestamps. It replaces manual sampling, where specialists listen to a small share of calls. In QODEQ AI Voice QA, calls are scored on 15 weighted criteria.

How many calls does manual QA usually cover?

At 5,000 calls a day, 1–2 specialists can review about 128 calls, which is roughly 2.6% coverage. Each call takes around 7.5 minutes to review manually. Full manual coverage at that volume would take 78 people.

How long does AI take to review one call?

Between 5 and 15 seconds per call, depending on length. A 4.5-minute call is typically reviewed in under 10 seconds. That makes 100% coverage possible without extra staff.

Which transcription technology is used?

QODEQ AI Voice QA uses Whisper to transcribe every inbound and outbound call into searchable text. The transcript is then scored against your criteria, and each finding is linked to the exact second in the recording.

Can it check responsible-gaming compliance on calls?

Yes, as one of the scoring criteria. Your compliance team defines the protocol — what the agent must say and offer when a responsible-gaming trigger occurs — and every call is checked against it. Misses are flagged with a timestamp.

Can we use our own call scripts and criteria?

Yes. The 15 criteria are weighted to your call scripts during week 2 of rollout and calibrated against your quality controllers in a parallel run in week 3. Inbound, VIP and outbound calls can have different scripts.

Does AI call QA replace our quality controllers?

It replaces manual sampling, not the people. The 1–2 specialists who spent their time listening move to calibration, coaching and trend analysis, and their re-checks keep the model aligned with your standards.

How is call data protected?

Recordings are processed locally, kept encrypted and handled in line with GDPR. Storage rules are agreed during telephony integration before any processing starts.

How much does AI Voice QA cost?

It is billed per checked call: $0.30 for 5,000–30,000 calls a month, $0.25 up to 100,000, $0.20 up to 200,000 and $0.18 above that, with no setup fee. At 5,000 calls a day that is about $1,000 a day, against roughly $18,720 for full manual coverage.

How is call QA related to chat QA?

Both use the same 15-criteria scoring model, so you get one quality standard across chats and calls. The chat product, AI QA System, is available now and scores 100% of chats with 97.6% accuracy.
