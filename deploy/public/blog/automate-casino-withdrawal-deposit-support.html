Why payment tickets deserve their own automation

Payment tickets deserve separate treatment because they are the most time-sensitive and the most data-dependent part of casino support: the player is waiting for money, and the answer lives in a payment system rather than in a help article.

A generic FAQ bot cannot tell a player where their €820 is. It can only say that withdrawals “usually take up to 24 hours”, which is exactly what an anxious player does not want to hear. So the conversation goes to an operator. The operator opens the back office, then the PSP dashboard, compares references, writes an answer and often creates a ticket for the payments team. Manual handling takes 3–5 minutes of operator time per ticket and costs about $0.50.

Players judge a brand by how payments feel, so these tickets also carry more emotional weight than a question about game rules. A slow or vague answer here is how a routine status check becomes a complaint, a chargeback or a lost depositor.

The good news is that most of these tickets follow a small set of patterns. Once the assistant can read the same data an operator reads, the patterns can be resolved end to end.

Deposits that “didn’t arrive”, including p2p

A deposit that was not credited is the hardest case to answer by hand, because the operator has to find the matching transfer. With p2p transfers, the payer’s name rarely matches the player’s account. The assistant matches every ticket against PSP data the moment it arrives, so the transfer is found even without a clear payer reference. Only genuine exceptions reach a human.

Pending withdrawals

Most “where is my withdrawal?” tickets are not problems at all: the payout is approved and simply moving through the provider. The assistant reads the live status, explains where the money is and when it should arrive, and closes the ticket. If a payout is actually stuck, it opens the ticket and fills it in for your payments team itself.

Crypto deposits

Crypto adds its own pattern: deposits sent to the wrong address or network. The assistant handles these through the flow you define, so the player gets a clear next step instead of waiting in a queue.

How the assistant works with PSP data and your helpdesk

The assistant connects directly to your PSPs and internal APIs, so every answer is based on the live transaction rather than a template.

  1. The ticket arrives. Usually it comes from the chat, where the AI Chat Operator recognises a payment question and opens the ticket.
  2. PSP data is pulled. Transaction information comes straight from the payment system: status, amount, method and timestamps.
  3. The case is matched and classified. The deposit is matched to the transfer and the withdrawal is checked against its settlement window. Receipts are checked for authenticity.
  4. The player gets an answer. A typical status question is answered in about 10 seconds, in the player’s language.
  5. The ticket is resolved or escalated. Routine cases are closed. Exceptions are created in the helpdesk with all data attached and passed to the right team.

The integration with the Chat Operator matters more than it looks. The chat bot opens the ticket and the assistant picks it up inside the same conversation, so the player never repeats themselves. There is no “please fill in this form” and no second channel. Your payments team receives a ticket that already contains the transaction, the status check and the reason for escalation.

Time to take a payment ticket into work drops from 8:00 to 0:35. Even the tickets that do reach a human arrive pre-investigated.

PDF receipt forgery checks

Receipt checks protect the operator from paying out against a fake document: the assistant analyses uploaded PDF receipts and flags forged ones before a payout is approved.

Players upload bank receipts to prove deposits, especially for bank transfers and p2p payments. Checking them by eye is slow, and a well-made fake is easy to miss when the queue is long. The assistant runs an AI authenticity check on every uploaded receipt. If the check passes and the transfer matches PSP data, the ticket is resolved. If something looks wrong, the case goes to a human with the reason attached.

What stays with humans

Anything that involves money at risk, a dispute or a judgement call stays with your team. The assistant’s job is to make sure those cases arrive complete, not to decide them.

  • Chargeback threats. These are escalated to risk with the payment history and the player’s claim.
  • Duplicate charges. Both transactions are identified and passed on, and refunds are decided by people.
  • Suspected fraud and failed receipt checks. These go to the relevant team with the check result.
  • Cases outside your rules. You set escalation rules and fraud thresholds during calibration, and the assistant follows them.

This split is how the assistant can be involved in 90% of payment tickets while resolving 70%+ of them alone. The remaining tickets still benefit, because nobody has to start the investigation from zero.

Checklist: is your payment support ready to automate?

You are ready to automate if you can give the assistant read access to transaction data and a clear set of rules for when a human must step in.

  • List the PSPs you use and start with the ones that generate the most tickets.
  • Prepare API access and documentation for your PSPs and helpdesk. This is what makes a 2–3 day rollout possible.
  • Write down settlement windows per method (cards, e-wallets, bank transfer, crypto) so the assistant can tell “normal” from “stuck”.
  • Define escalation rules and fraud thresholds: chargebacks, duplicates, large amounts, failed receipt checks.
  • Decide where tickets land: which helpdesk queues, which teams, which fields must be filled.
  • Connect the chat. If players ask about payments in live chat, pair the assistant with the AI Chat Operator so the handoff stays in one conversation.
  • Plan how you will check quality. Payment answers should be audited like any other conversation; see our guide to support QA in iGaming.
